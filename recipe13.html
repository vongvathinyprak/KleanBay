Pepper Lunch

A Japanese-style sizzling rice bowl with thin sliced beef, sweet corn, and onion in a savory garlic sauce, topped with butter and black pepper.

Serves 2 • 5 main ingredients + sauce

Ingredients

  • 200 grams of thinly sliced beef
  • 1/2 onion, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup of corn kernels, canned (drained) or frozen
  • 2 cups of cooked rice, warm
  • 1 tablespoon of butter

Sauce

  • 2 tablespoons of soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon of oyster sauce
  • 2 cloves of garlic, minced
  • 1 tablespoon of honey
  • 1 teaspoon of sugar
  • 1 pinch of salt and a few grinds of black pepper

Equipment

  • Large non-stick pan or skillet
  • Small bowl for the sauce
  • Spatula
  • Cutting board and knife

Instructions

  1. In the small bowl, stir together the soy sauce, oyster sauce, garlic, honey, sugar, and salt until the sugar dissolves. Set it aside.
  2. Divide the warm rice between two plates or heat-safe bowls. If you are using frozen corn, thaw it in the microwave for 1 minute first.
  3. Heat a little oil in the pan on medium-high. Cook the onion for 2 to 3 minutes until it softens, then add the corn and cook for 1 more minute. Move them onto the rice. Ask an adult to help with the hot pan.
  4. In the same pan, cook the beef for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until there is no pink left. Pour in the sauce and stir for 1 minute until it bubbles and coats the beef.
  5. Spoon the beef and sauce over the rice. Top each serving with half of the butter and finish with lots of black pepper. Mix everything together while it is hot, and let it cool for a minute before eating.

Ingredient Notes

Contains soy, wheat (in soy sauce), milk (butter), and shellfish (oyster sauce). Honey is not suitable for babies under 1 year old. Cook the beef until there is no pink left, to 71°C (160°F) if you have a thermometer. Wash your hands and tools after touching raw meat, and refrigerate leftover rice within 1 hour or so.

Make It Your Own

Add a fried egg, shredded cabbage, or chopped green onion on top. For the real sizzle, serve it on a hot cast-iron plate, but only with an adult handling it.
