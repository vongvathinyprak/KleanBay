Korean Popcorn Chicken

Crispy, golden fried chicken bites tossed in a sweet, sticky, mildly spicy Korean-style glaze.

Serves 2 • 6 main ingredients

Ingredients

  • 300 grams of boneless, skinless chicken breast or thigh, cut into bite-size pieces
  • 1/2 cup of cornstarch
  • 2 cups of vegetable or canola oil, for frying
  • 2 tablespoons of gochujang (Korean chili paste)
  • 1 tablespoon of honey
  • 2 tablespoons of soy sauce (1 for the chicken, 1 for the sauce)

Equipment

  • Deep, heavy pot or deep frying pan
  • Cooking thermometer (recommended)
  • Tongs and a slotted spoon
  • Large bowl and small bowl for the sauce
  • Plate lined with paper towels

Instructions

  1. Pat the chicken dry. Mix it with 1 tablespoon of the soy sauce and a pinch of pepper, and let it sit for 10 minutes. In a small bowl, stir the gochujang, honey, and the other tablespoon of soy sauce together to make the sauce.
  2. Toss the chicken in the cornstarch until every piece is fully coated, pressing the starch on with your hands. Shake off the extra.
  3. Ask an adult to help with this step and stay with you the whole time. Pour the oil into the pot so it is about 3 to 4 cm deep and never more than halfway up the sides. Heat on medium until it reaches about 175°C (350°F). If you have no thermometer, a piece of coated chicken should sizzle steadily when it goes in.
  4. Using tongs, lower a few pieces at a time into the oil, placing them away from you so oil does not splash. Fry for 3 to 4 minutes, turning once, until golden brown and crispy. Do not crowd the pot.
  5. Move the chicken to the paper towel plate to drain. Break one piece open to check that there is no pink inside. It should reach 74°C (165°F) if you have a thermometer. Let the oil heat back up before the next batch.
  6. Toss the hot chicken in the sauce and sprinkle with sesame seeds and green onion. Let it cool for a few minutes before eating. Turn off the heat and leave the oil until it is completely cold before moving or throwing it away.

Ingredient Notes

Frying uses very hot oil and should only be done with an adult helping. Never leave hot oil alone, keep water away from it, and pat the chicken dry so it does not spit. If oil catches fire, turn off the heat and cover the pot with a lid. Never use water on it. Contains soy and usually wheat (in soy sauce and gochujang). Gochujang is spicy, so start with 1 tablespoon if you prefer mild food. Honey is not suitable for babies under 1 year old. Wash your hands, the bowl, and the cutting board after touching raw chicken.

Make It Your Own

Add a little minced garlic or a squeeze of lime to the sauce, or serve the chicken with rice and pickled cucumber.
